Anki Converter

Puts Anki decks on the reader without any sync: open an exported .apkg, pick decks, and copy the result to the SD card. Each card is drawn with its deck's own template, and you review it offline on the reader.

1. Open an Anki deck

In Anki: File → Export, choose Anki Deck Package (.apkg). Shared decks from AnkiWeb are .apkg files too. Everything stays in your browser.

2. Choose decks

A ticked deck includes its subdecks. Each ticked deck becomes one deck on the reader.

SD card folder

How to install

  1. Unzip the download at the root of the SD card. It holds .system/anki/<deck id>/; the .system folder can be hidden in your file manager, which is normal.
  2. Or upload the folders over Wi-Fi with the reader's File Transfer page.
  3. Open Flashcards on the reader: the decks are listed with your own.

Review progress stays on the reader; it does not go back to Anki. To keep Anki up to date, sync the reader with Anki on your computer instead. That needs no AnkiWeb either: see Offline reviews, synced with Anki on your computer in the reader's user guide.

Not on the reader: audio, typing in answers (the back shows the expected answer), scripts and buttons in a template. A deck keeps up to 2,000 cards.

Output matches tools/anki_import/apkg_to_flashcards.py from the matcha-reader firmware. Reads packages with sql.js and fzstd (MIT).
